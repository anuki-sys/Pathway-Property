There is a difference between a broker and a mortgage manager

See the comparison

Most people in the Islamic finance space go straight to a mortgage manager. That is one provider, one set of products, and no obligation to show you anything else.

We work differently. As a broker we hold a legal obligation called Best Interest Duty, which means we compare across at least three funders before we recommend anything. And because we are not tied to one provider, the recommendation follows your situation rather than our panel.

The other difference is what happens after settlement. A mortgage manager processes your application and manages the account. We keep reviewing your position, because rates drift upward the longer you sit still.

How governance works at Sira

Every product we recommend has been reviewed for Shariah compliance before it reaches you. We can tell you what was reviewed, by whom, and what the certification covers. Where documentation exists, you can ask for it.

What we will not do is tell you something is halal. That is not our call to make. Our job is to explain exactly how a structure works - who owns what, what you are paying, and what your obligations are - so you can make that judgement with proper information.

The things people ask before they call

Is Islamic finance actually available in Australia?

Yes. The first Shariah-compliant provider in Australia has been operating since 1989, and the number of providers grew significantly from around 2019 onward. There are now several funders and mortgage managers offering Islamic home finance nationally, which is exactly why comparing them properly matters.

How is Islamic home finance different from a conventional home loan?

A conventional arrangement charges interest on money lent to you. Islamic finance avoids interest entirely and uses ownership-based structures instead. The most common in Australia is Ijarah, which is a lease-to-own arrangement where the funder holds ownership and you make payments that build toward full ownership.
