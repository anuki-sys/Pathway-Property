# The Melbourne Buyer's Digest - content and structure options

> **Decided.** Voice is roughly **75% voice 03 plain and direct** with
> **25% voice 02 warm and supportive**, warmth rationed to three named places.
> Structure is **B, The Companion**. Both are now built into `issue-01.html`,
> and the operating rules are in the README. This document is kept as the
> record of what was considered and why.

Three voices and four structures, written from the same facts so they can be
compared directly. Nothing here is built into `issue-01.html` yet.

---

## Voices

### 01. Serious and professional

*Reads like a brief from a firm that knows things. Credibility first.*

- **Best for.** Best if the list skews analytical, or if you want the Digest to sit beside the contract review work as evidence of rigour.
- **Risk.** Lowest warmth of the three. On a cold list it can read as distant, and it makes the reply CTA harder to earn.

### 02. Warm and supportive

*A person who was there on Saturday, telling you what it meant.*

- **Best for.** Best for a first-home audience and for building a list that replies. Strongest at making someone feel less alone in the process.
- **Risk.** Needs discipline. Warmth without a hard fact in every section becomes reassurance with nothing under it.

### 03. Plain and direct

*No warmth, no formality. Useful, unfussy, over quickly.*

- **Best for.** Best for completion rate and for the busiest readers. Cheapest to write well every week, which matters at 52 issues a year.
- **Risk.** Carries the least brand personality. Easy to mistake for any other buyer agency sending a weekly.

## The same six elements in each voice

### Subject line and preheader

*The first decision. Everything else is read through it.*

**01 Serious and professional**

**Melbourne clearance rate falls to 55%**

What a softer auction market means for buyers in the south east corridor.

**02 Warm and supportive**

**One in two homes didn't sell on Saturday**

Which means your weekend just got a little less frantic.

**03 Plain and direct**

**55%. Here's what to do about it.**

Three homes worth your Saturday, and what Officer actually costs.

### The opening

*Where the voice is set. Same four auctions in Officer, three ways.*

**01 Serious and professional**

Melbourne cleared 55% of reported auctions in the week to 5 September, against 71% in the same week a year earlier. The south east corridor is tracking below the metropolitan average, with Officer, Pakenham and Clyde North all recording higher pass-in rates than in the first half of the year.

The practical consequence for buyers is straightforward. Roughly one campaign in two now concludes without a sale on the day, which moves the negotiating position from the vendor to the remaining buyer.

**02 Warm and supportive**

I stood at four auctions on Saturday. Two of them didn't sell.

A year ago that would have been unusual. Now it is most weekends, and I want to tell you what it actually means, because nobody standing on that footpath explains it at the time. When a home passes in, the campaign has not failed. It has turned into a conversation, and the person still standing there is the one who gets to have it.

If house hunting has felt relentless lately, this is the week the pace changed.

**03 Plain and direct**

Half of Melbourne's auctions didn't sell last weekend. A year ago it was a quarter.

What to do with that: stop treating auction day as the deadline. Two of the four I watched on Saturday passed in, and both sold within the hour under the quoted range. The buyer still standing at 11am did better than anyone who bid.

That is the whole lesson this week.

### The market section

*The hardest one to get right. Same two figures, same RBA date.*

**01 Serious and professional**

**55%** cleared, week to 5 September, preliminary
**71%** the same week a year earlier

Preliminary rates are revised as late results are reported, so the figure should be read as a direction rather than a precise number.

The Reserve Bank meets on 29 September. All four major banks now forecast a further increase before year end. Buyers holding a pre-approval written at current assessment rates should confirm their position with their broker before committing to a price.

**02 Warm and supportive**

Just over half the homes that went to auction in Melbourne last weekend sold on the day. Last year it was closer to three in four.

**55%** sold on the day
**71%** the same weekend last year

That is not a market falling apart. It is a market where you get to think.

**Worth a phone call this week.** The Reserve Bank meets on 29 September and rates might move. Ask your broker what your budget looks like either way, so the number in your head is the real one. Certainty is what makes open homes feel manageable.

**03 Plain and direct**

**55%** cleared. **71%** a year ago. Preliminary, so read it as a direction.

RBA meets 29 September, and all four majors expect a rise before Christmas.

One job this week: ask your broker what your approval is worth if the rate moves. Do it before you fall for something.

*One CTA, a reply in all three. The ask is identical, the invitation is not.*

## Structures

### A. The Brief

**About 450 words, 2 min** &middot; pairs with Serious and professional

1. The market, with the two figures and what they mean
2. Suburb in focus, with what actually sold there
3. Three homes worth a Saturday
4. What's on, three lines, no images
5. Reply

- **Why.** Market-led and tight. Nothing decorative, no personal note, no light moment. The credibility comes from being the shortest useful thing in the inbox.
- **Production cost.** Cheapest to produce. No voice note needed from Tanuj, so it survives a week when he is travelling.
- **Trade-off.** Builds authority faster than affection. Hard to grow a replying list from it.

### B. The Companion

**About 900 words, 4 min** &middot; pairs with Warm and supportive

1. A note from Tanuj, written from his own voice note
2. What this means for you
3. Three we'd walk through
4. What it's like to live in [suburb]
5. One we had to show you
6. Your weekend
7. On the pod
8. Reply

- **Why.** Person-led. Opens on someone who was physically there, and every section after it is information that person is handing you. This is the structure the current build uses.
- **Production cost.** Most expensive. Needs the voice note every week, three listings researched, and a suburb written up. Breaks first when the week gets busy.
- **Trade-off.** Longest, so completion drops. The payoff is that it is the only structure that makes a stranger feel known.

### C. The Shortlist

**About 500 words, 2 min** &middot; pairs with Plain and direct

1. Five homes worth your Saturday, with a price guide and one line each
2. What sold last weekend, so the guides can be checked
3. The market in three lines
4. Reply

- **Why.** Listings-led. The homes are the product and everything else supports them. Most forwarded of the four, because a shortlist is a useful thing to send your partner.
- **Production cost.** Moderate, and it is the same job every week, which makes it the most sustainable at 52 issues.
- **Trade-off.** Least brand personality. Reads like a service rather than a voice, and it is the easiest for a competitor to copy.

### D. The Two-Parter

**About 300 words, 90 seconds** &middot; pairs with Any of the three

1. One thing that happened, the market read in about 120 words
2. One thing worth doing, which rotates weekly: a suburb, a shortlist, an explainer, or a reader question
3. Reply

- **Why.** Radical restraint. One observation, one useful thing, gone. The rotating second half means the format never goes stale while the production load stays flat.
- **Production cost.** Lowest by a distance, and the only one of the four that is genuinely sustainable at 52 issues with a small team.
- **Trade-off.** Gives up the room to show range. No featured listings most weeks, and the podcast has nowhere to sit.

## Recommendation

Send **B** while the list is small and you are still earning the first replies,
then move to **D** once the weekly load starts slipping.

The honest constraint is not which one reads best. It is that the Digest is
weekly, and the structure that survives week 30 is the one worth choosing now.
B is the most persuasive and the most fragile; it needs a voice note, three
researched listings and a suburb write-up every single week. D asks for one
observation and one useful thing.

On voice, **02 warm and supportive** fits the brief you gave me and fits the
reply CTA, which needs someone to feel invited rather than briefed. The risk to
manage is discipline: every warm section still needs a hard fact under it, or it
turns into reassurance with nothing behind it. If the list turns out to skew
toward investors rather than first-home buyers, **01** is the safer register and
pairs with **A**.
