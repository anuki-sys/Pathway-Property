The headline

Cash rate, held
29 Sep
Next RBA call, 2:30pm
 
 The cash rate is 4.35%, unchanged since the August meeting and effective from 12 August. The RBA next decides on Tuesday 29 September at 2:30pm - 13 days after this email lands.
 
 The Bank has already raised three times this year, 75 basis points in total. Headline inflation eased to 3.5% in the year to July, down from 3.8% in June, but the trimmed mean held at 3.6%. Both still sit above the 2 to 3% target.
 
 All four major banks now expect another rise before the end of the year. NAB has it at September. ANZ, CBA and Westpac have it at November.
 
What this means if you're buying this weekend
You have more room than you did a year ago. A 55% clearance rate means roughly one in two auctions does not sell under the hammer. Passed-in properties are negotiations, and the buyer still standing afterwards has leverage the underbidders never get.
Check your pre-approval expiry before you bid. With a live chance of a rise on 29 September, an approval written at today's assessment rate may not stretch as far in October. Ask your broker what your number becomes at a 4.6% cash rate before you commit to a price.
Do not read a soft market as licence to lowball everything. Good stock in the corridor is still competitive. The softness is uneven, and it is concentrated in properties with a compromise attached.

Good to know
The cooling off trap at auction
 
 The right exists, and it is short. Under section 31 of the Sale of Land Act 1962, you can end a residential contract within 3 clear business days of the day you sign. You give the vendor or their agent written notice.
 
 It is not free. You get everything back except $100 or 0.2% of the purchase price, whichever is greater. On an $800,000 home that is $1,600.
 
 The clock starts when you sign, not when the vendor does. Vendors often sign days later. That does not extend your window.
 
 The trap: auctions have no cooling off at all. There is none if you buy at a publicly advertised auction or on the day it is held, and none if you buy within 3 clear business days either side of one. The bid is final.
 
 It also does not apply if the property is used primarily for industrial or commercial purposes, if it is over 20 hectares and used primarily for farming, if you and the vendor previously signed a contract for the same land on substantially the same terms, or if you are an estate agent or a company.
  The point: cooling off is a safety net for private sales, and it does not exist at auction. Every check you were going to do afterwards has to be done before you raise your hand - contract reviewed, finance confirmed, building and pest read.
