Strategy of the month
Rooming houses
One property let room by room to individual tenants (in VIC, 4+ unrelated residents), producing several rents from a single title instead of one.
Why we're focusing on it now
■ Cashflow: typically neutral to positive from day one - rare in today's rate environment
■ Rental demand: single renters, students and key workers are the most squeezed segment of the rental market
■ Diversification: balances a growth-heavy portfolio that's eating into serviceability
Three ways we buy them
Established, already operating $900k+
Income from settlement. Growth is average and depends on location.
 
Convert an older, larger home $700k+
1-3 years of negative cashflow during works, then strong income on refinance.
 
New build rooming house $1.3M+
Low maintenance, strong yield. Lower growth, as most of the capital goes into the build.
Suits
Clients who need income now, or whose borrowing capacity is capped by negatively geared properties
Clients with $[#]k+ in deposit or equity who are comfortable with a more hands-on asset
Doesn't suit
Clients chasing maximum capital growth or wanting a "set and forget" asset
Clients relying on negative gearing for tax benefits
What it means for your clients
Brokers
Lender appetite is narrower: fewer lenders, often lower max LVRs and rooming-house-specific valuations. We flag lending structure before we shortlist.
Accountants
Strong rental income changes the tax picture. Review the ownership structure and depreciation (fit-out, fire services) before purchase.
Financial planners
A cashflow asset that can support serviceability for the next purchase. Fits clients moving from accumulation toward income.
Conveyancers
Extra checks on council registration, building classification, fire safety and minimum standards compliance. We do this due diligence before contracts are signed.
